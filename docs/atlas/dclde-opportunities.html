DCLDE 2027 for science and art: what the labels can support#

Checked 7 October 2026 from the downloaded files, not from descriptions. This report feeds three of the new proposals: orca populations, fin-whale moveout and the right-whale census. It extends the general DCLDE guide, which covers challenge status, downloads and the public orca models.

Every number below is regenerated by one script and saved with input hashes in resources/audits/dclde-opportunities.json:

uv run main.py dclde metadata                # full annotation CSV, if not present
uv run python dclde_opportunities.py fetch   # DAS picks, localizations, aerial CSV, DORI labels
uv run python dclde_opportunities.py audit

The fetch adds about 15 MB: 120 DAS arrival-pick CSVs, the authors' batch-1 localization table, the 21-row aerial survey and DORI's 13 MB label table. No audio, NetCDF detections or selection tables were downloaded.

Population classification: the labels carry provider conventions#

The DC-2 task asks for population, especially Southern Resident (SRKW) versus Transient/Bigg's (TKW). The combined CSV supports that question only partly.

NRKW exists only as detections, and all SAR calls come from one provider (UAF_NGOS). The more important finding concerns the frequency boxes:

SIO supplies 53.6% of call-level TKW annotations (2,207 of 4,121). Its boxes have zero bandwidth, with a median at 180 Hz. Pooled across providers, TKW calls therefore appear to sit at a median upper bound of 791 Hz against 4.9 kHz for SRKW. That difference is an annotation convention, not a population difference: within JASCO_VFPA_ONC, TKW and SRKW boxes reach similar medians (7.7 and 7.5 kHz). Any method that uses box geometry, box-cropped spectrograms or pooled bandwidth statistics would learn provider practice.

Where both populations were annotated in the same dataset, a site-controlled comparison is possible. Encounter days, not calls, are the independent units:

Only the first three call-level datasets have at least two days of each population. That is enough for a leave-one-day-out pilot, not for population claims. The audit JSON keeps the full table.

A finer label layer exists outside the combined CSV. DORI's label table (59,442 rows; prerelease, partially verified) contains 2,775 human annotations of 28 SRKW call types, led by S1 (1,216), S44 (242), S19 (231), S36 (216) and S16 (202). A research agent also reports per-call call types inside individual DCLDE provider folders, for example N-, T- and OFF-calls in DFO_CRP and S-calls in SIMRES. Those provider files were not re-counted here.

Related published evidence: Palmer et al. (2026) report that SRKW/TKW models degrade on a different hydrophone system and that generalization depends on call-type balancing (DOI 10.1111/mms.70126). The two-stage SPARROW paper splits by sound file, not by provider (arXiv:2609.01792). The box-convention confound above was not mentioned in either abstract.

Each call is a set of distance/time pairs along one cable: a moveout curve whose apex marks the cable point nearest the whale. That shape is directly usable as a drawing. It is not yet a whale position: localization needs association across picks and cables, a sound-speed model and an uncertainty estimate.

The authors' repository adds batch1_localizations_with_coords.csv: 176 localizations in ten minutes (02:00–02:10 UTC), labelled by sensor (north, south, hf, lf) and type (hf, lf, pair), all at a fixed depth of −30 m. Its deltax column has undocumented units. Treat it as a published research output, not the challenge's scoring reference. The JASA paper reports association recall of 0.92–0.94 with precision near 0.3 (Goestchel et al. 2026, DOI 10.1121/10.0044257). Raw DAS strain is not in the challenge bucket; it comes from OOI RAPID.

Abundance: 21 flights against a whole season of calls#

The aerial table has 21 flights, 20 February – 16 May 2019, with total individuals per flight from 0 to 129 (7 April). Effort varies: 5–15 track lines and 3 h 11 min – 9 h 14 min over the area. There are no individual positions. The 118 daily selection tables (336 MB) contain candidate upcall detections with scores and channels; NOAA states that false positives remain and supplies no calling rate.

Garcia, Tolkova et al. already calibrated acoustic cue counts against concurrent aerial surveys, after TDOA de-duplication, and found more calling at night (ESR 56:101–115, 2025). A new contribution therefore has to ask something else: whether one calibration holds across the season. With 21 flights that is a small, falsifiable test, set out in P5.

What this means for the proposals#

Track Labels that exist What we can responsibly test What we cannot claim
DC (population) Call boxes by provider, ecotype, day; DORI SRKW call types Whether SRKW/TKW separation survives within one site and held-out days; whether provider is more decodable than population Dialect or meaning; population claims from pooled boxes
L (spatial) Arrival picks; ten minutes of research localizations Moveout geometry; how one versus two cables constrain position Tracks or counts of individual whales without association
DE (abundance) 21 aerial counts; daily candidate detections Stability of calls-per-whale across the season Abundance from call counts without detection and cue-rate models
