# DCLDE 2027 for science and art: what the labels can support

Checked **7 October 2026** from the downloaded files, not from descriptions.
This report feeds three of the [new proposals](novel-projects.md):
[orca populations](novel-experimental-plans.md#p3-population-voices-without-recorder-fingerprints),
[fin-whale moveout](novel-experimental-plans.md#p4-moveout-fin-whale-calls-drawn-by-a-fibre) and
[the right-whale census](novel-experimental-plans.md#p5-calls-against-counts-a-right-whale-census-hourglass).
It extends the general [DCLDE guide](dclde-2027.md), which covers challenge
status, downloads and the public orca models.

Every number below is regenerated by one script and saved with input hashes in
[`resources/audits/dclde-opportunities.json`](../resources/audits/dclde-opportunities.json):

```bash
uv run main.py dclde metadata                # full annotation CSV, if not present
uv run python dclde_opportunities.py fetch   # DAS picks, localizations, aerial CSV, DORI labels
uv run python dclde_opportunities.py audit
```

The fetch adds about 15 MB: 120 DAS arrival-pick CSVs, the authors' batch-1
localization table, the 21-row aerial survey and DORI's 13 MB label table. No
audio, NetCDF detections or selection tables were downloaded.

## Population classification: the labels carry provider conventions

The DC-2 task asks for population, especially Southern Resident (SRKW) versus
Transient/Bigg's (TKW). The combined CSV supports that question only partly.

| Killer-whale rows | SRKW | TKW | NRKW | SAR | OKW | Unknown |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `Call` level | 14,240 | 4,121 | 0 | 8,078 | 1,495 | 5,900 |
| `Detection` level | 6,668 | 8,586 | 8,266 | 0 | 1,203 | 550 |

NRKW exists only as detections, and all SAR calls come from one provider
(UAF_NGOS). The more important finding concerns the frequency boxes:

| Provider | KW rows | Boxes with LowFreqHz = HighFreqHz | Rows without frequency bounds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| SIO | 2,638 | **2,638** | 0 |
| SIMRES | 3,578 | **3,578** | 0 |
| OrcaSound | 1,730 | 0 | **1,730** |
| JASCO_VFPA_ONC | 1,886 | 5 | 0 |
| Six other providers | 49,275 | 0 | 0 |

SIO supplies **53.6% of call-level TKW annotations** (2,207 of 4,121). Its
boxes have zero bandwidth, with a median at 180 Hz. Pooled across providers,
TKW calls therefore appear to sit at a median upper bound of 791 Hz against
4.9 kHz for SRKW. That difference is an annotation convention, not a population
difference: within JASCO_VFPA_ONC, TKW and SRKW boxes reach similar medians
(7.7 and 7.5 kHz). Any method that uses box geometry, box-cropped spectrograms
or pooled bandwidth statistics would learn provider practice.

Where both populations were annotated in the same dataset, a site-controlled
comparison is possible. Encounter days, not calls, are the independent units:

| Provider / dataset | Level | SRKW calls (days) | TKW calls (days) |
|---|---|---|---|
| JASCO_VFPA_ONC / StraitofGeorgia | Call | 1,263 (17) | 248 (5) |
| ONC / BarkleyCanyon | Call | 128 (2) | 350 (5) |
| SIO / Cpe_Elz | Call | 83 (5) | 1,928 (29) |
| JASCO_VFPA / HaroStraitSouth | Call | 2,658 (12) | 57 (1) |
| JASCO_VFPA / BoundaryPass | Call | 988 (7) | 47 (1) |
| DFO_WDLP / five sites | Detection | 6,620 (22 site-days) | 749 (9 site-days) |
| DFO_CRP / WVanIsl | Detection | 48 (4) | 5,334 (56) |

Only the first three call-level datasets have at least two days of each
population. That is enough for a leave-one-day-out pilot, not for population
claims. The audit JSON keeps the full table.

**A finer label layer exists outside the combined CSV.** DORI's label table
(59,442 rows; prerelease, partially verified) contains **2,775 human
annotations of 28 SRKW call types**, led by S1 (1,216), S44 (242), S19 (231),
S36 (216) and S16 (202). A research agent also reports per-call call
types inside individual DCLDE provider folders, for example N-, T- and OFF-calls
in DFO_CRP and S-calls in SIMRES. Those provider files were not re-counted here.

Related published evidence: Palmer et al. (2026) report that SRKW/TKW models
degrade on a different hydrophone system and that generalization depends on
call-type balancing ([DOI 10.1111/mms.70126](https://doi.org/10.1111/mms.70126)).
The two-stage SPARROW paper splits by sound file, not by provider
([arXiv:2609.01792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01792)). The box-convention
confound above was not mentioned in either abstract.

## Spatial sound: arrival picks trace a curve, not a position

| DAS fin-whale annotation | Value |
|---|---:|
| Annotation CSVs (5 batches × 2 cables) | 120 |
| Arrival picks | 11,117 |
| Annotated calls (`call_id` within file) | 794 |
| High- / low-frequency call types | 470 / 324 |
| Median picks per call | 14 |
| Median moveout across a call's picks | 6.8 s |
| Median cable span covered by a call | 16.4 km |
| UTC span | 2021-11-03 22:09 – 2021-11-04 16:16 |

Each call is a set of distance/time pairs along one cable: a moveout curve whose
apex marks the cable point nearest the whale. That shape is directly usable as
a drawing. It is not yet a whale position: localization needs association across
picks and cables, a sound-speed model and an uncertainty estimate.

The authors' repository adds `batch1_localizations_with_coords.csv`: **176
localizations in ten minutes** (02:00–02:10 UTC), labelled by sensor (`north`,
`south`, `hf`, `lf`) and type (`hf`, `lf`, `pair`), all at a fixed depth of −30 m.
Its `deltax` column has undocumented units. Treat it as a published research
output, not the challenge's scoring reference. The JASA paper reports
association recall of 0.92–0.94 with precision near 0.3
([Goestchel et al. 2026, DOI 10.1121/10.0044257](https://doi.org/10.1121/10.0044257)).
Raw DAS strain is not in the challenge bucket; it comes from OOI RAPID.

## Abundance: 21 flights against a whole season of calls

The aerial table has **21 flights, 20 February – 16 May 2019**, with total
individuals per flight from 0 to 129 (7 April). Effort varies: 5–15 track lines
and 3 h 11 min – 9 h 14 min over the area. There are no individual positions.
The 118 daily selection tables (336 MB) contain candidate upcall detections with
scores and channels; NOAA states that false positives remain and supplies no
calling rate.

Garcia, Tolkova et al. already calibrated acoustic cue counts against concurrent
aerial surveys, after TDOA de-duplication, and found more calling at night
([ESR 56:101–115, 2025](https://doi.org/10.3354/esr01384)). A new contribution
therefore has to ask something else: whether one calibration holds across the
season. With 21 flights that is a small, falsifiable test, set out in
[P5](novel-experimental-plans.md#p5-calls-against-counts-a-right-whale-census-hourglass).

## What this means for the proposals

| Track | Labels that exist | What we can responsibly test | What we cannot claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| DC (population) | Call boxes by provider, ecotype, day; DORI SRKW call types | Whether SRKW/TKW separation survives within one site and held-out days; whether provider is more decodable than population | Dialect or meaning; population claims from pooled boxes |
| L (spatial) | Arrival picks; ten minutes of research localizations | Moveout geometry; how one versus two cables constrain position | Tracks or counts of individual whales without association |
| DE (abundance) | 21 aerial counts; daily candidate detections | Stability of calls-per-whale across the season | Abundance from call counts without detection and cue-rate models |
