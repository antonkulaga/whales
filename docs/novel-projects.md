# New science-and-art projects with whale and dolphin sound

Prepared **7 October 2026** for Livia's marine residency. Eight proposals, each
with a distinct scientific question, artistic outcome and data requirement,
ranked, with one pilot already run on this repository's assets.

**Recommendation.** Start with **Hardata II** (P1): a silver band that stores a
dolphin whistle as a readable groove, tested by asking a 2026 dolphin-trained
encoder whether the decoded sound still belongs to its whistle type. It needs no
training beyond a linear probe, ties directly to Livia's 2021 *Hardata*, and the
pilot has produced a clear first answer: [pilot report](hardata-ii-pilot.md).
The next two are **Expectation in humpback song** (P2) and **A whistle alphabet
from sparse features** (P7).

### Why these three

**P1 Hardata II** tests a published biological claim (identity in contour) on a
2026 species-trained representation, and turns the answer into a material
constraint. It is the most direct continuation of Livia's own work: the one
piece where she engraved sound, and the one problem she named, that it could not
be played. Everything it needs is local.

**P2 Expectation in humpback song** asks whether the July 2026 humpback model
has learned song or texture, a question its authors have not answered, and
reuses the human-versus-model game design Livia built for glucose forecasting.
The data download is 55 MB plus a checkpoint.

**P7 Whistle alphabet** fills a verified gap (no sparse-autoencoder study of a
bioacoustic encoder was found) and yields a modular vocabulary that matches how
Livia already designs: repeated modules with controlled variation.

## The pilot and what it showed

The pilot is P1's minimal experiment, run on 420 held-out OpenWhistle test
whistles with the cached OpenWhistle encoder, a generic DAC codec and a
simulated casting channel. All five registered predictions held:

| Inscription | Bits per whistle | Type retained (macro-F1) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original recording | ~585,000 | 0.82 |
| Pitch contour, 32 points × 8 bits | 272 | 0.62 |
| Same contour reversed, or flattened to its mean pitch | 272 / 24 | 0.23 / 0.26 |
| Spectrogram relief | 528 / 2,320 | 0.51 / 0.66 |
| DAC codec, 1 / 4 codebooks | 728 / 2,888 | 0.21 / 0.59 |
| Amplitude only (the 2021 Hardata rule) | 112 | 0.10 (chance ≈ 0.17) |
| Contour groove read from a simulated cast band, 0.1 mm blur | analog | 0.61 |

The dolphin-trained encoder keeps three quarters of its whistle-type performance
from the pitch contour alone, and it uses the contour's shape. An exploratory
probe trained on decoded tones raises the 272-bit contour to 0.75 (91% of
original), level with a codec using ten times the bits. A general-audio
model (CLAP) does not: it falls to chance on contour decodes. The contour is the
only inscription measured that both works and fits a band at cast silver's
0.35 mm detail. The groove simulation is optimistic; casting and scanning real
bands is the next step. Full results: [pilot report](hardata-ii-pilot.md#results);
playable gallery generated at `data/output/inscription/index.html` (local,
Git-ignored).

## Keeping three kinds of claim apart

| Proposal | Acoustic pattern | Biological interpretation | Artistic correspondence |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | F0 contour, spectrogram cells, codec tokens | Signature-whistle shape carries identity information (playback evidence) | Time around the band, pitch across it |
| P2 | Per-token surprisal over codec tokens | Song is repeated phrases and themes; meaning unknown | Fold depth follows surprise |
| P7 | Sparse feature activations | None assumed; features are tested against contour descriptors | One cast module per feature |

Probe accuracy in P1 measures one encoder's separation of whistle types. It is
not evidence of what a dolphin would hear; a partnered playback study would be.

## Decisions for Livia

1. Which whistle types become bands, and whether a set should include the
   shared non-signature type alongside named dolphins' signature types.
2. Band dimensions and whether to engrave the groove on the outside, the inside
   (private) or both. The pilot assumed an 18 mm inner diameter, 6 mm width.
3. Whether to cast three test bands now (the next P1 step) and who scans them.

## Further seeds, not developed here

- **Two voices in one call:** a 2026 Norwegian orca set (Zenodo 22956877) marks
  F0, harmonics and biphonation as pixel masks; a double helix (Livia's Helix
  wedding rings) could follow the two sources.
- **Coda vowels:** Beguš et al. (2025, 2026) report vowel-like spectral classes
  in sperm-whale codas, with a small labelled table but no audio.
- **Song locked to dives:** Zeh et al. (2026) relate humpback song cycles to
  dive profiles; no public audio with tags was found.
- **A decade of song:** MBARI's continuous Pacific Sound archive (since
  2015) could become a spiral of years, after Ryan et al. (2025).
