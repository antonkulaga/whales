# Existing projects: cetacean sound, music and drawing

Investigated **7 October 2026**. This is a selected review of documented projects,
not a claim to have found every artwork. An artist's project page establishes
what the installation does; its biological interpretation needs separate evidence.
Older precedents are included here. The proposed implementation shortlist uses
2026 releases, audited in the companion report.

The music and sound-to-visual categories already have precedents. In the sources
reviewed here, I did **not** identify a demonstrated dolphin-operated acoustic
painting interface with evidence that the animal learned to control the drawing.
That is a scoped search result, not proof that nobody has ever attempted one.

## Seeing Echoes in the Mind of the Whale

Marshmallow Laser Feast's 2024 installation combines hydrophone recordings,
underwater footage and scientific collaboration. Its described pipeline uses
machine learning to help identify vocalizations and a custom generative pipeline
to extract and reconstruct volumetric scenes from footage. Auditory data then
manipulates the environment through a real-time renderer.

![Seeing Echoes installation, official project photograph](https://marshmallowlaserfeast.com/app/uploads/2024/10/A7400185-1024x683.jpg)

Photograph from the [official project page](https://marshmallowlaserfeast.com/project/seeing-echoes-in-the-mind-of-the-whale/).
The authors describe the result as a speculative rendition of sculpting with
sound. It is an artistic interpretation, not a validated decoder of whale vision.

**Lesson for this project:** sound-reactive generative visuals existed in 2024.
Our useful extension is to expose the causal connection: select an audible
phrase, see its extracted contour or learned units, and inspect which folds or
openings they change in Livia's object. A newer model alone does not establish
an advance in explanation or animal participation.

See [the sound brush](2026-art-ideas.md#2-dolphins-drawing-through-sound-and-a-human-keyboard-demo)
and [the model audit](2026-art-models.md#what-counts-as-a-2026-model).

## Tay Fins

The collaboration between Fanny Lam Christie and the Sea Mammal Research Unit
connects public sculpture to the bottlenose dolphins of the Tay Estuary. Three
bronze dolphins incorporate markings of known animals. Surrounding stones carry
spectrograms of local dolphin vocalizations, and the project provides routes to
information about individuals and to listening. The reviewed description does
not say that a sound model generated the bronze bodies.
[SMRU project page](https://dolphins.wp.st-andrews.ac.uk/tay-fins/).

**Lesson for Livia:** the link between a tangible object, an identifiable animal,
its recording and its place can carry the work. A whistle-derived silver gesture
could have a recording and annotation attached to it, while retaining the design
problem of the setting: exposing amber, draining around opal or making an open
lattice. This is a closer material precedent than an arbitrary image of a whale.

See [map-to-sound navigation](2026-art-ideas.md#4-connect-the-map-to-audio-and-annotations)
and the [world-map guide](world-map.md).

## Dolphins painting with Wyland

Dolphin Research Center describes a repeated collaboration: a dolphin makes
marks first, Wyland studies them and develops a composition, then shows the
canvas and invites additional marks before finishing and signing both names.
The announcement's page metadata dates it to 28 October 2021. It establishes
participation in painting; it does not establish that the dolphin intended the
final representational scene.

![Wyland and a dolphin painting, official Dolphin Research Center photograph](https://dolphins.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/20211027_Wyland-Jax_SJM2_078-scaled.jpg)

[Primary account and photograph](https://dolphins.org/acclaimed-artist-wyland-collaborates-with-dolphin-research-center-dolphins/).
The center also documents its [Paint with a Dolphin program](https://dolphins.org/web/paint-with-a-dolphin/).

**Lesson for a sound brush:** make the contribution of each participant visible.
The source sound can establish a stroke; the human can choose a material,
arrangement or rendering. Keep the original stroke and the subsequent edit so
visitors can see the collaboration. Moving from a held brush to vocal control
would still require a new learned interface and evidence of control.

See [the human instrument and animal research distinction](2026-art-ideas.md#2-dolphins-drawing-through-sound-and-a-human-keyboard-demo).

## Whale music and live visuals

**No One Knows Why Whales Sing** premiered at Hudson Valley MOCA on 13 October
2018. Shawn Lawson's primary account credits David Rothenberg as composer,
Michael Gilbert Ronstadt on cello, Eric Despard on guitar and Lawson for live
coding visuals. It demonstrates that whale music and responsive visual
performance are established artistic territory.
[Collaborator's account and performance video](https://www.shawnlawson.com/portfolio/no-one-knows-why-whales-sing/).
Direct [Vimeo performance player](https://player.vimeo.com/video/295369037?app_id=122963&dnt=1).

Rothenberg also plays alongside whales and works with recordings. NJIT's 2020
account describes the **Whale Sounds** sample pack made with producer Umru,
including raw and musically sequenced recordings. This is an important baseline
for our first idea: a musician can already compose around actual animal sound
without a generative animal model.
[NJIT account](https://news.njit.edu/njits-david-rothenberg-offers-ocean-possibility-music-makers).

**Our proposed extension:** compare a musician-readable contour/timing guide
with direct source-audio conditioning in a 2026 music model. Retain the whale
track independently and demonstrate whether changing its phrase actually
changes the accompaniment. Model-generated continuation is an additional
creative layer; label it separately from the field recording.

See [music that follows the sound](2026-art-ideas.md#1-music-that-follows-whale-or-dolphin-sound)
and [the relevant model interfaces](2026-art-models.md#music-model-interfaces).

## Pattern Radio: Whale Songs

![Pattern Radio interface, official project image](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/B1OjwlfQUPIWA47JIpG22ZB9_4igQlkyjdsJ4ef3mClLzbX2AGXRjG-Zr_1rjgEwUOzT7ia2m6tJoe79kLH55I6G0ynsuiI)

NOAA and Google Creative Lab released Pattern Radio in June 2019. It visualizes
thousands of hours of humpback songs and uses AI to help listeners explore the
archive. Its official description credits marine researchers and artists and
lists WebGL, TensorFlow and the Web Audio API among its technologies.
[Project overview, images and launch link](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/patternradio).
Its primary page embeds [this overview video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JE3-LkMqBfM).

**Lesson for our map:** offer a navigable listening surface rather than just
geographic pins. Link a location to recordings, then to time intervals,
annotations and related sounds. A similarity view should let the visitor jump
back to the original recording. The proposed event index adds the provenance
needed to connect that listening experience to generated objects.

This precedent is an audio exploration tool. It does not establish that our
existing geographical points already contain playable per-event recordings.
See [the map proposal](2026-art-ideas.md#4-connect-the-map-to-audio-and-annotations).

## CHAT and underwater keyboards

CHAT is the closest reviewed precedent for the interaction mechanism. Wild
Dolphin Project and Georgia Tech describe a computer with hydrophones, an
underwater speaker and a keypad. Artificial whistles label play objects such as
sargassum, a scarf and a rope. Recognition tells the diver which programmed
whistle was heard; this is an interface, not translation of arbitrary dolphin
speech. Their account explicitly distinguishes a possible mimic from contextual
use or functional understanding.
[Primary project description](https://www.wilddolphinproject.org/our-research/chat-research/).

An earlier aquarium keyboard study with bottlenose dolphins reports spontaneous
mimicry of the artificial whistles. In its second year it also reports some productive,
context-appropriate use of these facsimiles by the two males that used the keyboard.
It supports the possibility of learning a sound interface, not the claim that dolphins
can already draw vocally.
[PMID 8375147; DOI 10.1037/0735-7036.107.3.301](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8375147/).

The CHAT field outcome is published in
[Herzing et al. 2024, DOI 10.26451/abc.11.02.02.2024](https://doi.org/10.26451/abc.11.02.02.2024).
Wild spotted dolphins imitated the computer-generated whistles, immediately and with
delay, but "did not show signs of a functional understanding of object labels."
[Herzing's 2016 review of interfaces and keyboards](https://doi.org/10.12966/abc.04.11.2016)
traces the same distinction back to 1960s acoustic systems.

**Proposed transfer:** replace play-object requests with a small, perceivable set
of drawing effects. First build and test the human-operated version. A subsequent
animal study would need to distinguish control of an outcome from imitation,
incidental vocalization and responses to the researcher.

See [the drawing experiment](2026-art-ideas.md#2-dolphins-drawing-through-sound-and-a-human-keyboard-demo)
and [its evidence ledger](2026-art-models.md#evidence-ledger).

The [detailed CHAT report](chat-sound-interface.md) adds hardware photographs,
the interaction loop, DolphinGemma's role and the 2025 whistle-controlled
vehicle prototype. That prototype's project page describes live dolphin trials
as future work, so it is a design precedent rather than demonstrated learned
animal control.
[Georgia Tech capstone](https://expo.gatech.edu/prod1/portal/portal.jsp?c=17462&g=413665329&id=417265676&p=413142918).

## The CymaScope image claim

The 2016 SpeakDolphin/CymaScope paper is relevant because it claims object-like
patterns from echolocation recordings. Its methods describe processed playback
into a water cell and frame selection; it is not an available neural decoder
with held-out target reconstruction tests.
[Primary paper, DOI 10.4172/2155-9910.1000202](https://www.speakdolphin.com/pressrelease/Speak-Dolphin_Phenomenon-discovered-while-imaging-dolphin-echolocation-sounds-2155-9910-1000202.pdf).

Echo-object recognition has a firmer experimental basis, including matching
after listening to another dolphin's inspection. That concerns returning echoes
from a physical target. It does not demonstrate deliberate transmission of a
stored visual image.
[DOI 10.3758/BF03199007](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03199007).

The appropriate follow-up is [a controlled echo-to-shape study](2026-art-ideas.md#3-images-in-dolphin-sound),
with known targets and acquisition geometry. Calling an attractive generated
picture a decoded dolphin image would go beyond the evidence.

## What these precedents change in our brief

1. Make audible source-to-effect relationships inspectable; responsive imagery
   alone is already well established.
2. Use Livia's real material questions and forms as the design vocabulary.
3. Keep each participant's contribution available: recording, mapping, human
   choice and generated result.
4. Treat learned animal control as an experimental question distinct from a
   successful human art demo.
5. Connect objects to recordings and places without inventing precise locations
   or meanings absent from the source data.

The [idea report](2026-art-ideas.md) turns these lessons into first artifacts,
comparisons and stop conditions. The [model report](2026-art-models.md) records
what 2026 releases can actually supply.
